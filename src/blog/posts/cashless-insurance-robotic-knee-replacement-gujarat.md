  <h2 style="color: #1a6fc4; font-size: 1.55rem; margin-top: 44px; border-left: 4px solid #1a6fc4; padding-left: 14px;">Pre-Authorization: What Happens 24 Hours Before Surgery</h2>

  <p>Pre-authorization (pre-auth) is the process by which your insurer formally approves your planned surgery before you are admitted. Without pre-auth, cashless treatment is not possible. At DHS, this process is handled entirely by our TPA coordination team — you do not need to interact with the insurer directly.</p>

  <p>Here is the pre-auth sequence at DHS:</p>

  <ol style="line-height: 2.3; padding-left: 22px; color: #333;">
    <li><strong>2–3 days before surgery (for planned elective cases):</strong> DHS billing desk initiates pre-auth with your insurer/TPA. Documents submitted include: hospital estimate, surgeon's letter of medical necessity, pre-operative investigation reports (X-rays, blood tests, ECG).</li>
    <li><strong>Within 12–24 hours:</strong> Insurer responds with approval, partial approval, or query. DHS's TPA coordinator responds to queries and follows up directly — you do not need to make calls.</li>
    <li><strong>If partial approval:</strong> DHS billing team explains the approved amount versus any self-pay component in writing, before surgery commences. No surprises at discharge.</li>
    <li><strong>On surgery day:</strong> Pre-auth approval letter is in hand. Cashless admission proceeds. No upfront payment (beyond co-pay or deductible, if applicable in your policy).</li>
  </ol>

  <div style="background: #e8f4fd; border-left: 4px solid #1a6fc4; border-radius: 6px; padding: 16px 20px; margin: 24px 0;">
    <strong>Important:</strong> Cashless pre-auth approval does not mean 100% of all charges are covered. It means the insurer has agreed to pay the approved amount directly to DHS. Any co-pay, deductible, or excluded items will be clearly communicated to you in advance.
  </div>
